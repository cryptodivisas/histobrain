## Overview

The Beatles were an English rock band formed in Liverpool by John Lennon, Paul McCartney, George Harrison, and Ringo Starr. During the 1960s they developed from a hard-working live act into a studio-focused group whose songwriting and recordings changed popular music. Their combination of memorable songs, distinctive personalities, and constant experimentation produced worldwide cultural influence.

## Key Facts

- Core members: John Lennon, Paul McCartney, George Harrison, and Ringo Starr
- Managed by Brian Epstein and produced for most of their career by George Martin
- Landmark albums include *Revolver*, *Sgt. Pepper’s Lonely Hearts Club Band*, and *Abbey Road*
- Public touring ended in 1966, allowing greater studio experimentation

## History and Career

The group developed its stagecraft during long engagements in Liverpool and Hamburg. “Love Me Do” became their first British single in 1962, and rapid success led to the phenomenon known as Beatlemania. Their 1964 American television appearances accelerated the British Invasion. As the decade progressed, their music absorbed folk, Indian music, psychedelia, orchestral writing, and emerging studio technology. Business and personal tensions contributed to their breakup in 1970.

## Songwriting and Sound

Lennon and McCartney formed the central songwriting partnership, while Harrison increasingly contributed major songs. The band used close harmonies, strong melodic hooks, and changing instrumental textures. Tape loops, reversed sounds, varispeed recording, unusual instruments, and layered production turned the studio into part of the composition itself.

## Impact / Legacy

The Beatles helped establish the album as a unified artistic statement and expanded the creative authority of performers who wrote their own material. Their catalog remains commercially successful and continues to influence rock, pop, recording practice, fashion, and youth culture.

## Did You Know?

- Ringo Starr joined shortly before the group’s first single was recorded.
- The final scheduled commercial concert took place at San Francisco’s Candlestick Park in 1966.
- Their 1969 rooftop performance became their last public performance together.
- George Martin’s orchestral knowledge was crucial to many studio arrangements.
