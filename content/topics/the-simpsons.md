## Overview

*The Simpsons* is an American animated sitcom created by Matt Groening. It follows Homer, Marge, Bart, Lisa, and Maggie Simpson in the fictional town of Springfield. Through the family and a vast supporting cast, the series satirizes work, school, religion, politics, advertising, entertainment, and everyday family life.

## Key Facts

- Began as shorts on *The Tracey Ullman Show*
- Debuted as a half-hour series in 1989
- Developed by Matt Groening, James L. Brooks, and Sam Simon
- Became the longest-running American scripted primetime series

## History and Development

Groening created the family shortly before meeting with producer James L. Brooks, naming most characters after members of his own family. The early shorts led to a Christmas television special and then a regular series. Its rapid cultural rise helped establish the Fox network and proved that primetime animation could attract an adult audience.

## Format and Structure

Episodes typically begin with a family or community problem that can branch into satire, parody, or an emotional story. Springfield’s flexible geography and enormous cast allow the show to examine almost any institution. Opening variations—including Bart’s chalkboard message, Lisa’s saxophone solo, and the couch gag—turn repetition into a space for invention.

## Impact / Legacy

The show influenced animated comedy, television writing, vocabulary, and public satire. Its earlier seasons are especially celebrated for balancing absurd jokes with recognizable family emotion. Its extraordinary longevity has also made it a record of changing technology, politics, and television itself.

## Did You Know?

- The characters were colored yellow partly to stand out while viewers changed channels.
- Springfield’s state is intentionally left ambiguous.
- Many supporting characters are voiced by a relatively small ensemble.
- “D’oh!” entered major English dictionaries.
