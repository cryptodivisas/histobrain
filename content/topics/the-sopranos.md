## Overview

*The Sopranos* is an American crime drama created by David Chase. James Gandolfini stars as Tony Soprano, a New Jersey mob boss who begins therapy after panic attacks. The series moves between organized crime, domestic life, and Tony’s sessions with psychiatrist Jennifer Melfi, examining a man who seeks insight without necessarily accepting moral change.

## Key Facts

- Aired for six seasons on HBO
- Created by David Chase
- Stars James Gandolfini, Edie Falco, and Lorraine Bracco
- Set largely in northern New Jersey

## History and Development

Chase drew from New Jersey life, crime-film traditions, and his relationship with his own mother when developing the series. HBO allowed serialized storytelling, adult language, violence, and ambiguity beyond the limits of broadcast television. The show gradually expanded its family and criminal ensembles while keeping Tony’s contradictory inner life at the center.

## Format and Structure

Therapy provides a recurring frame but not a simple path to redemption. Dreams, memory, popular music, and sudden violence reveal what ordinary conversation hides. Episodes can focus on mob business, marriage, parenthood, aging, or depression, often leaving conflicts unresolved. The contrast between suburban routine and criminal brutality prevents either world from feeling separate.

## Impact / Legacy

*The Sopranos* helped establish the modern prestige television drama and proved audiences would follow a deeply flawed protagonist over many years. Its writing and performances influenced later antihero series, while its ambiguous final scene remains one of television’s most debated endings.
