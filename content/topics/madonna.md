## Overview

Madonna is an American singer, songwriter, performer, and actor often called the “Queen of Pop.” Since emerging in the early 1980s, she has repeatedly changed her music and image while addressing themes of identity, sexuality, religion, fame, and personal freedom. Her ability to combine songs with fashion, choreography, and provocative visual storytelling became a model for modern pop careers.

## Key Facts

- Born Madonna Louise Ciccone in Michigan
- Broke through with the albums *Madonna* and *Like a Virgin*
- Major songs include “Like a Prayer,” “Vogue,” and “Into the Groove”
- Known for elaborate tours and continual artistic reinvention

## History and Career

Madonna moved to New York City in 1978 and worked as a dancer while participating in bands and the downtown music scene. Her early dance singles led to a recording contract and international fame. Through the 1980s and 1990s, albums such as *True Blue*, *Like a Prayer*, *Ray of Light*, and *Music* showed her willingness to absorb new electronic and club styles.

## Performance Style and Sound

Her catalog moves through dance-pop, electronic music, R&B, acoustic songwriting, and disco. Madonna treats the album, video, costume, and live show as parts of one visual concept. “Vogue,” for example, brought a dance style rooted in New York’s ballroom culture to a mass audience, while *Ray of Light* paired electronic production with reflective lyrics.

## Impact / Legacy

Madonna expanded the creative and business control expected of women in popular music. Her use of controversy opened debates about censorship, gender, religion, and sexual expression. Later pop artists have drawn from her approach to reinvention, touring, video, and self-authored celebrity.

## Did You Know?

- Madonna studied dance before becoming a recording star.
- “Like a Virgin” was written by Billy Steinberg and Tom Kelly.
- The “Vogue” video was directed by David Fincher.
- She was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2008.
