## Overview

*Breaking Bad* is an American crime drama created by Vince Gilligan. Bryan Cranston plays Walter White, an Albuquerque chemistry teacher who begins manufacturing methamphetamine after a cancer diagnosis. Working with former student Jesse Pinkman, Walter claims he is protecting his family while increasingly pursuing power, recognition, and control.

## Key Facts

- Aired for five seasons on AMC
- Created by Vince Gilligan
- Stars Bryan Cranston and Aaron Paul
- Set and filmed primarily in Albuquerque, New Mexico

## History and Development

Gilligan designed the series around transformation: its protagonist would change from an apparently ordinary, sympathetic man into someone dangerous. Cranston’s casting surprised viewers who knew him mainly from comedy. Strong critical response and later availability through streaming helped the audience grow across the show’s run.

## Format and Structure

The series uses serialized cause and effect, allowing small decisions to produce consequences many episodes later. Chemistry functions as both plot mechanism and metaphor for change. Cold opens often show unexplained images from the future, inviting viewers to reconstruct how the story will reach them. Wide desert landscapes contrast with cramped domestic and criminal interiors.

## Impact / Legacy

*Breaking Bad* became a defining example of television’s antihero drama and is praised for visual storytelling, performance, pacing, and long-form character development. It also produced the prequel-sequel series *Better Call Saul* and the follow-up film *El Camino*.

## Did You Know?

- Walter’s alias “Heisenberg” refers to physicist Werner Heisenberg.
- Albuquerque became central to the show after production moved away from its originally imagined location.
- Colors in costumes and production design often reinforce character changes.
- The title is a regional expression suggesting a turn toward defiance or wrongdoing.
