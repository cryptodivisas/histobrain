## Overview

*Friends* is an American sitcom created by David Crane and Marta Kauffman. It follows Rachel, Ross, Monica, Chandler, Joey, and Phoebe as they build careers and relationships in New York City. The series combines romantic storylines with ensemble comedy and the idea that close friends can function as a chosen family.

## Key Facts

- Aired for 10 seasons on NBC
- Stars Jennifer Aniston, Courteney Cox, Lisa Kudrow, Matt LeBlanc, Matthew Perry, and David Schwimmer
- Central locations include Monica’s apartment and Central Perk
- The finale aired in 2004

## History and Development

Crane and Kauffman developed the show around the period of life when friends can become more important than relatives in everyday experience. After its 1994 premiere, the chemistry of the six-person cast quickly became its defining strength. Long-running romantic arcs, particularly Ross and Rachel’s relationship, helped turn weekly episodes into a broader serialized story.

## Format and Structure

Most episodes balance an A-story with one or two smaller plots, allowing different pairs of characters to interact. Running jokes and personality contrasts create rhythm: Monica’s competitiveness, Chandler’s sarcasm, Joey’s confidence, Phoebe’s eccentricity, Ross’s intensity, and Rachel’s growth repeatedly collide. Multi-camera staging and a live studio audience give the dialogue a theatrical pace.

## Impact / Legacy

*Friends* became one of the most recognizable sitcoms of its era and remained popular through syndication and streaming. Its hairstyles, catchphrases, coffeehouse setting, and relationship plots influenced fashion and later ensemble comedies, while also attracting criticism for an idealized and limited representation of New York.

## Did You Know?

- The series was almost titled *Six of One*.
- Central Perk’s orange sofa became one of television’s best-known props.
- The cast negotiated collectively for equal salaries in later seasons.
- “The One…” became the standard pattern for episode titles.
