## Overview

Aretha Franklin was an American singer, songwriter, and pianist celebrated as the “Queen of Soul.” Her voice could be commanding, tender, rhythmic, and improvisational, often within a single performance. By joining gospel technique to soul, pop, jazz, and rhythm and blues, she created recordings that became both personal declarations and public anthems.

## Key Facts

- Grew up singing in the Detroit church led by her father, C. L. Franklin
- Achieved her defining commercial breakthrough at Atlantic Records
- Signature recordings include “Respect,” “Think,” and “Chain of Fools”
- First woman inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame

## History and Career

Franklin recorded gospel music as a teenager before signing with Columbia Records, where she explored pop and jazz material. Her move to Atlantic in 1966 connected her with musicians and arrangements that emphasized her gospel strengths. A run of major hits followed. Franklin remained active for decades, performing at presidential events and returning repeatedly to gospel, the foundation of her musical identity.

## Vocal Style and Structure

Franklin used melisma, call-and-response, improvised runs, and forceful rhythmic placement learned in church. She was also an accomplished pianist who could shape a performance from the keyboard. Background vocals often answer her lead lines, turning a studio recording into something resembling a live congregation.

## Impact / Legacy

Her 1967 version of Otis Redding’s “Respect” became associated with civil rights, women’s empowerment, and personal dignity. Franklin set a benchmark for popular singing and influenced soul, rock, gospel, R&B, and contemporary pop vocalists.

## Did You Know?

- Franklin rearranged “Respect” and added the famous spelled-out refrain.
- Her sisters Carolyn and Erma were also professional singers.
- She performed “My Country, ’Tis of Thee” at Barack Obama’s 2009 inauguration.
- She received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2005.
