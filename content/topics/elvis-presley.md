## Overview

Elvis Presley was an American singer and actor known as the “King of Rock and Roll.” His recordings and charismatic performances brought Southern musical traditions to a huge mainstream audience during the 1950s. Although he did not invent rock and roll, his fame helped make the genre a central force in international youth culture.

## Key Facts

- Born in Tupelo, Mississippi, and raised in Memphis, Tennessee
- Made his first major recordings at Sun Studio
- Early hits include “Heartbreak Hotel,” “Hound Dog,” and “Jailhouse Rock”
- Served in the U.S. Army from 1958 to 1960

## History and Career

Producer Sam Phillips paired Presley with guitarist Scotty Moore and bassist Bill Black in 1954. Their version of “That’s All Right” mixed country delivery with blues material and attracted regional attention. After moving to RCA Victor, Presley became a national television sensation. Military service interrupted his rise, and much of the 1960s was spent making films. His 1968 television comeback special restored his reputation as a dynamic live performer, followed by major Las Vegas engagements.

## Performance Style and Sound

Presley drew from gospel quartets, country singing, blues, and rhythm and blues. His flexible voice could move from intimate ballads to forceful rock performances. Onstage movement, clothing, and vocal phrasing challenged conservative expectations, while his band’s stripped-down guitar, bass, and rhythm created the excitement of early rockabilly.

## Impact / Legacy

Presley became one of the first truly global rock stars and changed music, fashion, film, and celebrity marketing. His career also remains part of a larger discussion about race and the music industry, because Black artists who shaped rock and roll often received less promotion and financial reward.

## Did You Know?

- Presley’s middle name was spelled Aron on his birth certificate.
- He had an identical twin brother who was stillborn.
- His 1968 comeback special was originally planned around a different television concept.
- Graceland, his Memphis home, became a major public museum.
