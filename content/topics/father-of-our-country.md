## Overview

“Father of Our Country” is one of the answers USCIS accepts when the 2025 civics test asks applicants to name something George Washington is famous for. The nickname draws together three distinct roles: command of the Continental Army, leadership at the Constitutional Convention, and service as the first president. A useful way to remember those roles is to ask who gave Washington power and what he did with it.

## From general to private citizen

The Continental Congress gave Washington command of the army during the Revolution. On December 23, 1783, after independence had been secured by treaty, he returned his commission to Congress in Annapolis. The sequence matters: the highest military commander came before the civilian body that had appointed him and gave up his authority. The resignation is often remembered as an early example of the principle that military power is subordinate to civilian government.

## From convention chair to president

Washington presided over the Constitutional Convention in Philadelphia in 1787. Delegates wrote and debated the Constitution, and state conventions decided whether to ratify it. Washington then became the first president under the new framework. He served two terms and left office in 1797. That choice helped establish a precedent that later presidents could follow. The constitutional limit on presidential elections came much later, in the Twenty-Second Amendment.

## Study takeaway

Washington presided over the convention, while James Madison helped shape its proposals and left a detailed record of the debates. Remember <em>command, convention, presidency</em> for Washington. If you are preparing for naturalization, check the official USCIS materials for the version of the test that applies to you.
