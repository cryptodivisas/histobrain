## How to hear an argument in a Mozart opera

Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart was born in Salzburg in 1756 and died in Vienna in 1791. He toured as a child performer and settled in Vienna in 1781 after working for the archbishop of Salzburg. His catalogue includes concertos, symphonies, chamber music, and works for the stage. One way to hear what makes his stage writing distinctive is to listen for characters who want different things while singing in the same scene. [International Mozarteum Foundation, life and work](https://mozarteum.at/en/wolfgang-amadeus-mozart)

## Begin with the room in The Marriage of Figaro

The opera premiered in Vienna in 1786. Lorenzo Da Ponte wrote the Italian libretto, based on the play by Pierre-Augustin Caron de Beaumarchais. In the opening scene, Figaro is pleased with the room that the Count has assigned him and Susanna. Susanna sees danger in its location. The disagreement shows how differently the same privilege can look to two people. As the opera develops, the servants have to work around a nobleman with more authority, and the Countess has her own reasons to help them. [Metropolitan Opera, the plot and creation of Figaro](https://www.metopera.org/discover/education/educator-guides/le-nozze-di-figaro/the-operas-plot-and-creation/)

## Listen to the second-act finale in stages

Start with the Count and Countess. Then notice how the scene changes as Susanna, Figaro, and still more characters take part. The finale builds by adding people with different information and competing goals. A revelation that helps one person can leave another scrambling to explain it. Follow one singer for a few lines, then listen to the whole ensemble. Mozart lets you hear a social situation from several perspectives at once. Plot tells you who has entered the room. The music shows how each entrance changes who has the upper hand.
