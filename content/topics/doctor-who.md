## Overview

*Doctor Who* is a British science-fiction television series centered on the Doctor, an alien Time Lord who travels through time and space. The Doctor’s ship, the TARDIS, looks like a blue British police box but is vastly larger inside. Human and alien companions join adventures that combine history, science fiction, horror, comedy, and moral choice.

## Key Facts

- First broadcast by the BBC in 1963
- The Doctor can regenerate into a new body
- The TARDIS travels through time and space
- Famous adversaries include the Daleks, Cybermen, and the Master

## History and Development

The program began as family television intended to mix educational historical stories with imaginative science fiction. When actor William Hartnell left, regeneration allowed Patrick Troughton to take over the role without ending the show. The original run continued until 1989. A 1996 television film preceded a successful 2005 revival that brought the series to a new global audience.

## Format and Structure

Regeneration makes change part of the premise: each Doctor has a different personality while retaining memories and values. The TARDIS allows stories to move between distant planets, historical moments, and contemporary Earth. Earlier adventures often unfolded across weekly serial chapters; modern seasons more often combine individual episodes with a continuing mystery.

## Impact / Legacy

*Doctor Who* became a durable part of British culture and one of television’s longest-running science-fiction properties. Its flexible format survives changing casts, budgets, audiences, and production styles. The series is particularly known for turning limited resources into memorable ideas and monsters.

## Did You Know?

- The TARDIS exterior remained a police box because its camouflage system became stuck.
- The Daleks appeared during the program’s first season.
- Lost episodes survive partly through audio recordings made by viewers.
- The title character is the Doctor, not “Doctor Who” within most dialogue.
